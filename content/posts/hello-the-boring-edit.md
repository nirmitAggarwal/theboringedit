Every personal website is a promise to the future. This one is mine: a quiet
corner of the internet where I document what I'm learning, what I'm building,
what I'm reading, and what I can't stop thinking about. No algorithm, no
engagement metrics, no hot takes written to expire in a week — just notes,
set in warm ink, that I'll be able to reread in five years without cringing
(too much).

My name is **Nirmit**, and this is The Boring Edit.

![A desk with a lamp, coffee and a sheet of paper](/images/desk.svg "fig. 01 — the desk, where every post starts as a plain markdown file")

## What lives here

The site is organised around a few recurring kinds of writing:

- **Articles** — proper write-ups on code, systems and things I've gone deep
  on. The technical meat of this site.
- **Journey** — a running log of where I am: what I'm working on, what I'm
  stuck on, and what changed since the last entry. Honest, not curated.
- **Roadmaps** — the plans themselves. Numbered series that unfold part by
  part, so you can follow along — or catch up later, in order.
- **Resources** — the books, articles, tools and links worth keeping. If it's
  on this site, it earned its place.
- **Philosophy** — the why behind the what. Opinions about building, learning
  and living with software, held loosely enough to be corrected.
- **Interesting things** — the loose ends: a demo, a quote, a chart, a bug
  that taught me something. The stuff that doesn't fit a category but
  deserves to exist somewhere.

None of these require perfection. This is a notebook, not a newspaper.

## Why "boring"

The name is deliberate. *Boring* is what happens when a thing is understood,
reliable, and calm — and those are exactly the qualities I want in the
software I build and the words I write. The most durable technology is
boring. The best writing doesn't shout. A boring edit is the version of a
paragraph you reach after cutting everything that was only there to impress.

> The flashiest thing on this page is a single loud orange. Everything else
> is paper, ink, and hairlines.

So this site will stay deliberately small: fast static pages, a dot grid for
texture, no trackers, no pop-ups, no newsletter pop-over asking for your
soul. Just RSS if you want to follow along the old-fashioned way.

![A winding dotted route with numbered waypoints](/images/journey.svg "fig. 02 — the route: one post at a time, in order")

## How this will work

The writing here is plain **markdown files** on my machine. A small build
step compiles them into this site — math typeset, code highlighted, every
page prerendered to static HTML. When I write something new, the host simply
gets new pages added to it. There's no CMS behind this, no database, no
server quietly billing me by the hour.

A few commitments to make this worth visiting:

1. **Series over scattered posts.** Bigger topics — a language, a system, a
   deep skill — will run as *tracks*: numbered parts you can binge from
   part 01, regardless of when each part was written.
2. **Learn in public.** Half-finished understanding is welcome here, clearly
   labelled as such. Corrections are welcome even more.
3. **Write for the reread.** Every post should still make sense in a year —
   to you, and to future me.

## The philosophy, stated once up front

If the site has a constitution, it's these three lines:

- **Slow is smooth.** Depth beats frequency. I'd rather ship one post that
  still matters in a year than thirty that don't survive the week.
- **Own the Means.** The words live in markdown files I control, compiled to
  pages I host, on a domain I own. If a platform dies, nothing here dies
  with it.
- **Show the work.** Roadmaps include the wrong turns. Resources include the
  ones I abandoned and why. A journey log with only wins is a highlight reel,
  not a journey.

![A bookshelf of resources with one loud orange book](/images/shelf.svg "fig. 04 — the shelf: resources that earned their place")

## Where to start

- If you want the ongoing story, the **Journey** entries will accumulate
  here over time — this post is entry zero.
- Big topics will appear as **Tracks** on the tracks page, each part numbered
  so you can pick up exactly where you left off — the site even remembers
  what you've read, locally, in your own browser.
- The **shelf** will grow as resources earn their place.

That's the tour. First real entries are on the way — see you at part one.
